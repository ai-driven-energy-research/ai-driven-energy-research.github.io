\section{How Decisions Work}

\begin{takeaway}
\textbf{Decision rule requested for this meeting.} Let $N$ be the number of eligible editors participating in a vote and $A$ the number voting \textbf{against}. The motion \textbf{passes when $A\leq N/2$}; it fails only when \textbf{more than half} vote against. Abstentions are not votes against, so a tie passes. A recused editor is removed from $N$ for that motion.
\end{takeaway}

For every motion, the chair reads the exact wording below; the proposer has 20 seconds to clarify it; questions are limited to 30 seconds; then each participating editor votes \textbf{for}, \textbf{against}, or \textbf{abstain}. The public minutes record $N$, all three totals, the result, owner, deadline and evidence link.

\textbf{Authority after a pass.} The named proposer becomes the owner and may, on AIDER's behalf, make the website/GitHub changes, contact people and organise the work expressly described in the motion. A pass does \emph{not} authorise contracts, expenditure, fees, sharing private data, promising publication or authorship, changing the journal's legal/publisher identity, or committing another organisation. Any such step returns for a separate vote.

\textbf{Proposal cycle.} Proposals are normally introduced at one meetup and voted at the next. This gives everyone the same text and time to assess consequences. Today's V1--V7 motions come from the 13 June discussion record. \src{1} New proposals introduced today, including P8 in \S4, enter the third-meetup ballot.

\section{DOAJ: Requirements, Reality and Timeline}

The Directory of Open Access Journals is free to apply to. AIDER now has several foundations DOAJ expects: an online ISSN, immediate CC BY open access, five research articles, a dedicated website, a named editorial board and public author guidance. But an ISSN plus five papers is \textbf{not} enough. The current DOAJ Guide to Applying (v2.9) creates three binding gaps. \src{2}

\subsection{The three gates that control our application date}

{\small\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.22}
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{4.1cm} p{1.6cm} p{8.4cm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Requirement} & \textbf{Status} & \textbf{What it means for AIDER} \\
\midrule
Publishing history $>1$ year \emph{or} $\geq10$ OA research articles; at least 5 per year & \partialx & First publication was March 2026 and five articles are live. Five more clear the article-count gate, but do not solve the next two gates. \\
\addlinespace
At least two independent reviewers for every article; peer reviewers should be outside the journal's editorial team & \atrisk & Public issue records show the five papers received one or two human reports, but those reviewers were AIDER board members. Automated/AI reports are explicitly labelled ``not peer review.'' The process must change before the next article. \src{2,3,4} \\
\addlinespace
Editor/board/reviewer-authored papers $\leq25\%$ (latest two issues, or last calendar year for continuous publication) & \atrisk & All five current papers have at least one AIDER editor/board author: \textbf{5/5 = 100\%}. Reaching ten with five external papers would still be 50\%. If assessed across 2026, five internal papers require at least \textbf{20 total papers}---15 additional eligible external-author papers---to reach 25\%. \src{2} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}}

\begin{warnbox}
\textbf{The main correction to the earlier briefing.} A human editor who reads AI-generated reports is not a substitute for DOAJ's two independent reviewers. DOAJ currently treats its detailed AI section as best practice rather than a basic criterion, but it says reviewers and editors should not use generative AI to create assessments. The defensible AIDER model is: \textbf{automated reproducibility and integrity checks + two external human reports + a human editorial decision}. \src{2,4}
\end{warnbox}

\subsection{Website and operating work that can start immediately}

Before applying, AIDER must make the following easy to reach from the homepage: open-access policy; aims and scope; board names and affiliations; author instructions; exact editorial/peer-review process; licensing and copyright terms; an explicit statement that all author charges are zero; and publisher name, address, contact person and dedicated journal email. Licensing should appear in every article. A plagiarism/similarity process, archiving, repository policy, persistent identifiers and metadata deposits strengthen the application even where DOAJ labels them recommendations rather than basic criteria. \src{2}

Publish an AI-use policy that separates permitted automated checks from confidential human assessment. Recruit a real external reviewer pool before accepting more papers. For board-authored submissions, use a handling editor and reviewers with no conflict. Do not try to cure endogeny by creating artificial issues or by lowering quality to hit a number.

\section{New Proposal for the Third Meetup: Collaborate with JGPPS}

The journal remembered from the supervisor discussion is most likely the \textbf{Journal of the Global Power and Propulsion Society (JGPPS)}. Prof. Budimir Rosic is listed on its editorial board. JGPPS began publishing open access in 2017, entered DOAJ in August 2018, is preserved in Portico, and reports Web of Science/ESCI coverage for articles from 2020 and Scopus coverage from 2021. \src{6,7,8}

This makes JGPPS a strong potential mentor and collaborator in AIDER's overlapping area of AI, energy, turbomachinery and propulsion. It does \textbf{not} create an indexing shortcut: DOAJ and Clarivate evaluate AIDER itself. JGPPS cannot transfer its DOAJ, Scopus or Web of Science status to AIDER, and the same paper cannot be presented as independently published in both journals. Clarivate applies 24 journal-level quality criteria before entry to ESCI. \src{9}

\subsection*{P8 --- motion to circulate now and vote at the third meetup}

\begin{takeaway}
\textbf{Proposer/owner: Jianou Jiang.} Authorise Jianou to ask Prof. Rosic for a warm introduction to the JGPPS Editor-in-Chief or journal administrator and hold one non-binding exploratory conversation. Explore: DOAJ/WoS mentorship; peer-review and metadata workflow lessons; a jointly hosted reproducibility workshop; reciprocal, conflict-managed reviewer referrals; and a possible collaboration in the journals' overlapping scope. Jianou will return with written options, benefits, obligations, costs and conflicts before any commitment is made.
\end{takeaway}

Questions the exploratory meeting must answer:
\begin{itemize}
\item Is JGPPS willing to mentor a new diamond-OA journal on policies, external peer review, Crossref metadata and Portico-style preservation?
\item Would a co-hosted AI-for-energy reproducibility workshop benefit both communities without confusing the two publication records?
\item Can reviewers or authors be referred under explicit consent and conflict-of-interest rules?
\item If a special issue or co-publication is suggested, which journal is the publisher of record, where is it indexed, what fees apply, and who owns the metadata and editorial decision?
\item What evidence from JGPPS's 2017--2020 path was decisive for DOAJ, Scopus and ESCI?
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Recommended scope:} begin with mentorship and one joint workshop, not a merger, journal transfer or promise of shared indexing.

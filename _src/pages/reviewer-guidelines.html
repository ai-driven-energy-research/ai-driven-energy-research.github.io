Peer review

All research and review articles in AI-Driven Energy Research are peer reviewed. This page describes the review process and gives instructions for reviewers.

Review process

  1. Initial checks. The editorial office checks scope, completeness and formatting. Automated technical checks confirm that the required statements are present, that cited references can be located, and that files and supplementary materials are complete. These checks support the editors; no editorial decision is made automatically.
  2. Editor assignment. The manuscript is assigned to a handling editor with relevant expertise who has no conflict of interest. Manuscripts authored by an editor are handled by another member of the board.
  3. Reviewer invitation. The handling editor invites at least two independent reviewers, chosen for their expertise. Author suggestions may be considered but are not relied on alone.
  4. Review. Reviewers are normally asked to return their reports within three weeks.
  5. Decision. The handling editor considers the reports and decides: accept, minor revision, major revision or reject. The decision and reports are sent to the authors.
  6. Revision. Revised manuscripts may be returned to the same reviewers.
  7. Publication. On acceptance, the review reports and author responses are published with the article.

Transparency

The journal practises open peer review: review reports are published alongside accepted articles. Reviewers may choose whether their name is shown. Confidential comments to the editor are not published.

Instructions for reviewers

Reviewer dashboard

Invitations are sent by email with a private link to the reviewer dashboard, where you can accept or decline, download the manuscript files and submit your report.

Before accepting an invitation

  • Accept only if the manuscript is within your expertise and you can report within the requested time.
  • Declare any potential conflict of interest, for example recent collaboration with an author, the same institution, or a competing interest in the work.

Confidentiality

Treat the manuscript and its materials as confidential. Do not share them or use them for your own work before publication.

Assessment criteria

  • Originality: is the contribution new and significant for the field?
  • Soundness: are the methods appropriate and correctly applied, and do the results support the conclusions?
  • Data and code: do the materials provided allow the main findings to be checked?
  • Clarity: is the manuscript clearly written and are the figures and tables informative?
  • Relevance: is the work within the scope of the journal?

Structure of the report

  1. A short summary of the manuscript and its main claims.
  2. Major comments: issues that affect the conclusions.
  3. Minor comments: presentation, clarity and small corrections.
  4. A recommendation: accept, minor revision, major revision or reject.
  5. Confidential comments to the editor (optional).
  6. A statement on any AI tools used in preparing the review.

Section-specific questions

The editor may invite you to comment on one section or one specific question rather than the whole manuscript, matched to your expertise. Please answer the question asked; other observations are also welcome.

Use of AI tools by reviewers

Reviewers may use AI tools to assist them, for example to check calculations, code or language. Reviewers remain responsible for the content of their report and must disclose any AI use in it. To protect confidentiality, avoid tools that retain submitted content or use it for training. The editors welcome a short description of how AI was used, including the prompts, as this helps the journal develop good practice.

Recognition

Reviewers are acknowledged by name on published review reports if they wish, and may request a certificate of reviewing from the editorial office.

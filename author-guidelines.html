Instructions for authors

These instructions apply to all manuscripts submitted to AI-Driven Energy Research. Please read them before submitting. Manuscripts that do not follow them may be returned to the authors before peer review.

Contents: 1. Scope and article types · 2. Preparing your manuscript · 3. Declarations · 4. Data, code and reproducibility · 5. Use of AI tools · 6. Submitting your manuscript · 7. After submission · 8. Fees and licensing

1. Scope and article types

The journal publishes work in which artificial intelligence is central to understanding, designing or operating energy systems. See aims and scope.

  • Research articles report original work. There is no fixed length limit, but manuscripts should be as concise as the content allows.
  • Review articles give a critical, balanced survey of a field. Authors are encouraged to contact the editorial office before writing one.

Submissions must be original and must not be under consideration elsewhere. Posting a preprint (for example on arXiv) before or during submission is permitted.

2. Preparing your manuscript

File format

For first submission, upload the manuscript as a single Word (.docx) or PDF file, with figures and tables placed in the text. Authors using LaTeX may upload the compiled PDF and, optionally, the source files as a .zip archive. Editable source files will be requested on acceptance.

Title page

  • A concise, informative title.
  • The full names of all authors, their affiliations and countries.
  • The name and email address of the corresponding author. ORCID identifiers are encouraged for all authors.

Abstract and keywords

An abstract of no more than 300 words that states the problem, the approach, the main results and their significance, without references. Provide three to six keywords.

Main text

Research articles normally contain an introduction, methods, results, discussion and conclusions. Other structures are acceptable where the work requires it. Number sections, equations, figures and tables. Use SI units.

Figures and tables

Figures should be legible at the printed size, with labelled axes and units. Each figure and table needs a self-contained caption. Colour is free of charge.

References

Any consistent reference style is accepted at first submission. Please include DOIs wherever available; the editorial office checks that cited works can be located.

3. Declarations

Place the following statements at the end of the manuscript, before the references. They are also requested in the submission form.

  • Data and code availability. Where the data and code can be accessed, or why access is restricted (see section 4).
  • Use of AI tools. Which AI tools were used, for what purpose and to what extent (see section 5). Write "No AI tools were used" if that is the case.
  • Competing interests. Any financial or personal relationships that could be seen to influence the work, or "The authors declare no competing interests."
  • Funding. All sources of funding, with grant numbers.
  • Author contributions. The role of each author, preferably using the CRediT taxonomy.
  • Acknowledgements (optional).

4. Data, code and reproducibility

The journal expects the results in a paper to be checkable. Authors should make available the code and data needed to reproduce the main findings, normally through a public repository (for example GitHub, Zenodo, Hugging Face or an institutional repository) with a persistent identifier on acceptance. Materials may also be uploaded with the submission as a .zip archive.

  • Where data cannot be shared, for example because of size, commercial agreements or privacy, explain this in the data availability statement and provide whatever can be shared: input files, processed or reduced data, a representative sample or synthetic data, and the scripts used for analysis.
  • For simulations with commercial solvers, provide the input and configuration files and the solver version.
  • A short script or set of instructions that regenerates the main figures and tables is strongly encouraged. Authors who keep their work in a GitHub repository may use the journal's paper template; see code repositories and team submissions.

Reviewers are asked to comment on whether the materials provided support the conclusions.

5. Use of AI tools

Authors may use AI tools for writing, coding, analysis or other parts of the research. Their use must be disclosed in the Use of AI tools statement: name and version of each tool, what it was used for, and how its output was checked. AI tools cannot be listed as authors; the authors take full responsibility for the content. Authors are welcome, but not required, to provide session records or logs as supplementary material. See also the journal's AI policy.

6. Submitting your manuscript

Manuscripts are submitted through the journal's online submission system. No account is needed. The system asks for the following, in this order:

  1. Manuscript upload: the main document (Word, PDF or a .zip archive). The title, abstract, authors and keywords are extracted automatically to pre-fill the form; please check every field.
  2. Type, title and abstract.
  3. Keywords and subject area.
  4. Authors and affiliations, including the corresponding author.
  5. Suggested and opposed reviewers (optional).
  6. Details and declarations: cover letter, data and code availability, use of AI tools, funding, competing interests and author confirmations.
  7. Review and submit.

Authors who maintain their work in a public GitHub repository may alternatively open a submission issue. The editorial process is the same.

7. After submission

  1. Initial checks. The editorial office checks scope, completeness and formatting. Automated checks confirm that required statements are present, that cited references can be located and that files are complete. These checks support the editors; they do not make editorial decisions.
  2. Peer review. Manuscripts that pass the initial checks are assigned to a handling editor and sent to independent reviewers. See peer review process.
  3. Decision. The handling editor decides to accept, request minor or major revision, or reject. Authors receive the reviewers' reports.
  4. Revision. Revised manuscripts should be accompanied by a point-by-point response to the reviewers.
  5. Publication. Accepted articles are published on the journal website, archived on Zenodo with a DOI and listed in the current volume. Publication is permanent; see publication permanence.

Tracking your manuscript. After submission the corresponding author receives an email with a private link to the author dashboard, which shows the current status, its history, the progress of peer review and, when available, the decision letter and reviewer reports. Revised manuscripts are uploaded through the dashboard. Authors may withdraw a manuscript there, or by writing to the editorial office, at any time before acceptance.

8. Fees and licensing

  • Fees: none. There are no submission, article processing or page charges.
  • Copyright: authors retain copyright.
  • Licence: articles are published under CC BY 4.0. Code is normally released under the MIT or Apache 2.0 licence and data under CC0 or CC BY 4.0, at the authors' choice.
